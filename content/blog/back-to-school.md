I graduated from college almost 8 years ago. Since then, I’ve mostly followed the philosophy of learning by doing and using my job and projects as opportunities to learn and grow.

This means that apart from language courses and your corporate workshop here and there, I haven't joined any form of organized learning. In a recent effort to invest more in my education and creativity, I will take my first course at SFPC ([The School for Poetic Computation](https://sfpc.study/)) this fall. I’ve been tinkering with the idea of attending SFPC since my college days, when I discovered the world of creative computation.

The course I will take is [Zine Making for the Web](https://sfpc.study/sessions/fall-26/zine-making-for-the-web). It seemed like the perfect fit for me since I've been making fanzines since I was 12 years old.

I started with simple 1-page foldable drawing collections that I would give around to my school friends and neighbors. Then, in college, I was part of a small independent publishing collective in the comic, DIY, self-publishing scene in Bogotá. My fondest memories are creating with my friends [Nandy](https://www.behance.net/Navapale) and [Fabian](https://trashypero.cool/) and exchanging publications with others at concerts and fairs.

Since the beginning, what has drawn me to this medium is the lack of permissions needed. Other types of publishing are associated with an institution backing up the idea that what you make is worth being out in the world. But zine-making is a reminder that we are free to create and share with others.

I’m interested in exploring self-publishing in a digital context, where more and more small, intimate spaces are disappearing in exchange for huge monopolies driven by sales, self-marketing, and endless growth.

I’m genuinely excited about being in a collective and creative learning environment again, and can’t wait to start getting my hands dirty.
